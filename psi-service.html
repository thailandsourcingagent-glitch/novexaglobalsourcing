Introduction

What Is a Pre-Shipment Inspection?

A Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI) is an on-site quality check carried out after production is complete and goods are packed for export, but before the shipment leaves the factory. Our inspector physically visits the facility to verify that what was ordered is what was actually produced — checking quantity, appearance, function, packing, and labeling against your specification.

It's the last checkpoint you have before your money is tied up in a container on the water. For buyers sourcing from China, Thailand, or elsewhere in Asia without a local team on the ground, PSI is how you get eyes on your order without getting on a plane.

Why This Service Matters

Catch Problems Before They Become Your Problem

Once a container is sealed and on a vessel, your options for fixing a defect are expensive and slow. PSI moves that discovery point back to where it's still cheap and fast to act.

  • ✕ Wrong quantity shipped, discovered only at your warehouse
  • ✕ Defective goods sitting in your inventory with no recourse
  • ✕ Mislabeled cartons causing customs delays or fines
  • ✕ Barcode mismatches rejected by retail buyers
  • ✕ Disputes with suppliers with no independent evidence either way

FAQ

Common Questions

When should I schedule a PSI? +
Once production is at least 80–100% complete and goods are packed for export — this gives the most accurate picture of what will actually ship.
How long does an inspection take? +
Most inspections are completed within a single working day on-site, with the report delivered within 24–48 hours after.
What happens if the goods fail inspection? +
You receive a detailed report explaining exactly what failed and why. You then decide with the factory how to proceed — rework, partial shipment, or hold — before the container is sealed.
Do you inspect for buyers who didn't source through Novexa? +
Yes. PSI is available as a standalone service even if we weren't involved in sourcing the order.
Which sampling standard do you use? +
Sample sizes and defect tolerances follow internationally recognized AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit) tables, matched to your order quantity and risk level.
